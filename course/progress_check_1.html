    1.

    An object is moving in such a way that its position \(s\) (in meters) at time \(t\) (in seconds) is given by the function \(s\left(t\right)=8{t}^{2}-10t+100\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    (i)

    Set up, but do not evaluate, the limit definition of the derivative that would find the derivative of the function at \(t=1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} \lim_{t\to{}1}\frac{s(t)-s(1)}{t-1}&= \lim_{t\to{}1}\frac{8{t}^{2}-10t+100-98}{t-1} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (ii)

    Clearly explain why the limit you set up in Part a will find the derivative of \(s(t)\) at \(t=1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using the two different ways we have defined the derivative, the limit to find \(s'(1)\text{,}\) the derivative of \(s\) at \(t=1\text{,}\) is
    \begin{align*} s'(t) &= \lim_{t\rightarrow 1}\frac{s(t)-s(1)}{t-1}\\ \text{or}&\\ s'(t) &= \lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{s(1+h)-s(1)}{h}\\ &= \lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{s(1+h)-s(1)}{(1+h)-1}. \end{align*}
    🔗
    The expressions \(\frac{s(t)-s(1)}{t-1}\) for any value of \(t\) other than \(1\text{,}\) and \(\frac{s(1+h)-s(1)}{(1+h)-1}\text{,}\) for any non-zero value of \(h\text{,}\) are the average rate of change between the points \((1,s(1))\) and \((t,s(t))\text{,}\) or between \((1,s(1))\) and \((1+h,s(1+h))\text{,}\) respectively, so the closer \(t\) is to \(1\) or the closer \(h\) is to zero, the closer the average rate of change is to being an instantaneous rate of change.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Evaluate the limit you found in Part a.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} \lim_{t\to{}1}\frac{s(t)-s(1)}{t-1} &=\lim_{t\to{}1}\frac{8{t}^{2}-10t+100-98}{t-1}\\ &=\lim_{t\to{}1}\frac{8{t}^{2}-10t+2}{t-1}\\ &=\lim_{t\to{}1}\frac{(8{t}-2)(t-1)}{t-1}\\ &=\lim_{t\to{}1}(8{t}-2)\\ &=(8\cdot 1{t}-2)\\ &=6. \end{align*}
    or,
    \begin{align*} \lim_{h\to{}0}\frac{s(1+h)-s(1)}{t-1} &=\lim_{h\to{}0}\frac{8(1+h)^{2}-10(1+h)t+100-98}{h}\\ &=\lim_{h\to{}0}\frac{8{(1+h)}^{2}-10(1+h)+2}{h}\\ &=\lim_{h\to{}0}\frac{8{(1+2h+h^2)}-8-10h}{h}\\ &=\lim_{h\to{}0}\frac{8+16h+8h^2-8-10h}{h}\\ &=\lim_{h\to{}0}\frac{6h+8h^2}{h}\\ &=\lim_{h\to{}0}\frac{h(6+8h)}{h}\\ &=\lim_{h\to{}0}(6+8h)\\ &=6. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find an equation for the tangent line to the graph of \(s(t)\) from Question 1 at the point \(t=1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The tangent line goes through the point \((1,s(1))=(1,98)\) and has slope \(s'(1)=6\text{.}\) Using the point slope form, an equation for the tangent line is
    \begin{equation*} y=6(t-1)+98. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    A company’s monthly revenue (money taken in) from car sales, \(R\) (in thousands of dollars), is a function of monthly advertising spending, \(a\) (in thousands of dollars). That is, for some function \(f\text{,}\) \(R=f(a)\text{.}\)
    🔗

    (a)

    Interpret the following in practical terms, paying close attention to units.
    \begin{equation*} f(80)=250. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Solution.
    When monthly advertising spending is \(80\) thousand dollars, monthly revenue is \(250\) thousand dollars.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    What does the company expect is true about the sign of \(f'\text{?}\) Why?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The sign should be positive, because you expect more spending on advertising to bring in more revenue.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    What does the statement \(f'(100)=2 \) mean in practical terms?
    🔗
    Solution.
    When monthly advertising spending is \(100\) thousand dollars, monthly revenue increases by \(2\) thousand dollars per additional thousand dollars spent on advertising.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    What does the statement \(f'(100)=0.5\) mean in practical terms?
    🔗
    Solution.
    When monthly advertising spending is \(100\)  thousand dollars, monthly revenue increases by \(0.5\)  thousand dollars per additional thousand dollars spent on advertising. That is, revenue increases by \(500\)  dollars per additional thousand dollars spent on advertising.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    Suppose the company currently spends about $100,000 per month on advertising.
    🔗

    (i)

    If \(f'(100)=2\text{,}\) should the company spend more or less than $100,000 on advertising?
    🔗
    Solution.
    If \(f'(100)=2\text{,}\) each $1,000 spent brings in an additional $2,000 in revenue, so additional spending makes sense.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (ii)

    If \(f'(100)=0.5\text{,}\) should the company spend more or less than $100,000 on advertising?
    🔗
    Solution.
    If \(f'(100)=0.5\text{,}\) each $1,000 spent brings in only an additional $500 in revenue, which means they are spending more on advertising than they bring in, so additional advertising is not advisable. The company should spend less on advertising.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    A water tank in the shape of a cone with the point upward with a base radius of \(6\) ft and a height of \(24\) ft, as shown in Figure 47, is filling at a constant rate of \(0.12~\text{ft}^{3}/\text{min}\text{.}\) The goal of this problem is to construct a model for the depth of water as a function of time.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the total volume of the tank in cubic feet.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The total height is \(24\)ft and the radius of the base is \(6\)ft, so the total volume is given by
    \begin{align*} V_{T}= &=\frac{\pi}{3}R^{2}H\\ &=\frac{\pi}{3}(6\text{ft})^{2}(24\text{ft})\\ &=\frac{\pi}{3}36\cdot24 \text{ft}^{3}\\ &=288\pi\text{ft}^{3}\\ &\approx{}904.7787\text{ft}^{3} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the volume of water in the tank (in cubic feet) when the tank is filled to a depth of \(2\) ft and when the tank is filled to a depth of \(4\) ft.
    🔗
    Solution.
    When the tank is filled to a depth of \(2\text{ft}\text{,}\) the empty space above the water is a cone with height \((24-2)\text{ft}\text{,}\) and the radius of its base is in the same proportion to the radius of the whole tank as \((24-2)\text{ft}\) is to the full height of the tank. That is,
    \begin{equation*} \frac{r}{6\text{ft}}=\frac{(24-2)\text{ft}}{24\text{ft}} \end{equation*}
    so,
    \begin{align*} r&=\frac{(24-2)\text{ft}{6\text{ft}}}{24\text{ft}}\\ &=\frac{(24-2)\text{ft}}{4}\\ &=\frac{22}{4}\text{ft} \end{align*}
    So the empty volume is
    \begin{align*} V_{\varnothing} &=\frac{\pi}{3}\left(\frac{22}{4}\text{ft}\right)^{2}(22\text{ft})\\ &=\frac{\pi}{48}\left(22\text{ft}\right)^{3}\\ &=\frac{\pi}{6}\left(11\text{ft}\right)^{3}\\ &=\frac{1331\pi}{6}\text{ft}^{3} \end{align*}
    and so, the volume of water is
    \begin{align*} V_{w}&=V_{T}-V_{\varnothing}\\ &=\pi\left(288 -\frac{1331}{6}\right)\text{ft}^{3}\\ &=\frac{397\pi}{6}\text{ft}^{3}\\ &\approx{}207.8687\text{ft}^{3} \end{align*}
    🔗
    When the tank is filled to a depth of \(4\text{ft}\text{,}\) the empty space above the water is a cone with height \((24-4)\text{ft}\text{,}\) and the radius of its base is in the same proportion to the radius of the whole tank as \((24-4)\text{ft}\) is to the full height of the tank. That is,
    \begin{equation*} \frac{r}{6\text{ft}}=\frac{(24-4)\text{ft}}{24\text{ft}} \end{equation*}
    so,
    \begin{align*} r&=\frac{(24-4)\text{ft}{6\text{ft}}}{24\text{ft}}\\ &=\frac{(24-4)\text{ft}}{4}\\ &=5\text{ft} \end{align*}
    So the empty volume is
    \begin{align*} V_{\varnothing} &=\frac{\pi}{3}\left(5\text{ft}\right)^{2}(20\text{ft})\\ &=\frac{\pi}{3}500\text{ft}^{3}\\ &=\frac{500\pi}{3}\text{ft}^{3} \end{align*}
    and so, the volume of water is
    \begin{align*} V_{w}&=V_{T}-V_{\varnothing}\\ &=\pi\left(288 -\frac{500}{3}\right)\text{ft}^{3}\\ &=\frac{364\pi}{3}\text{ft}^{3}\\ &\approx{}381.1799\text{ft}^{3} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Find a formula for the volume of water in the tank (in cubic feet) when the tank is filled to a depth of \(d\) ft.
    🔗
    Hint.
    The volume of water is the volume of the whole tank minus the volume of the empty cone (from the surface of the water to the top of the tank).
    🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    When the tank is filled to a depth of \(d\text{ft}\text{,}\) the empty space above the water is a cone with height \((24-d)\text{ft}\text{,}\) and the radius of its base is in the same proportion to the radius of the whole tank as \((24-d)\text{ft}\) is to the full height of the tank. That is,
    \begin{equation*} \frac{r}{6\text{ft}}=\frac{(24-d)\text{ft}}{24\text{ft}} \end{equation*}
    so,
    \begin{align*} r&=\frac{(24-d)\text{ft}{6\text{ft}}}{24\text{ft}}\\ &=\frac{24-d}{4}\text{ft} \end{align*}
    So the empty volume is
    \begin{align*} V_{\varnothing} &=\frac{\pi}{3}\left(\frac{24-d}{4}\text{ft}\right)^{2}(24-d)\text{ft}\\ &=\pi\frac{(24-d)^{3}}{48}\text{ft}^{3} \end{align*}
    and so, the volume of water is
    \begin{align*} V_{w}&=V_{T}-V_{\varnothing}\\ &=\pi\left(288 -\frac{(24-d)^3}{48}\right)\text{ft}^{3}\\ &=\pi\left(\frac{13824-(24-d)^3}{48}\right)\text{ft}^{3} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    On the blank grid in Figure 48, sketch a graph of the depth of water as a function of time. Your sketch does not need to be perfect, but it should reflect how the rate of change of depth is changing as the tank fills.
    🔗
    You may assume the tank is empty to begin.
    🔗
    Solution.
    
    Depth as a Function of Time
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    Find an expression for the volume of water in the tank as a function of time, \(t\text{,}\) in minutes. (You may assume the tank is empty to begin.)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The volume of water as a function of time is given by \(v(t)=(0.12t)\text{ft}^{3}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    Equate the volume in terms of time with the volume in terms of depth and solve for depth. This yields a formula for the depth as a function of time.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Equating volume as a function of time with the volume as a function of depth, we have
    \begin{align*} \pi\left(\frac{13824-(24-d)^3}{48}\right)\text{ft}^{3} &=0.12t \end{align*}
    Solving this for \(d\text{,}\) we have
    \begin{align*} \pi\left(\frac{13824-(24-d)^3}{48}\right)=0.12t &\longrightarrow \left({13824-(24-d)^3}\right) =(0.12t)\frac{48}{\pi}\\ &\longrightarrow -(24-d)^3=1.8335t-13824\\ &\longrightarrow (24-d)^3=13824-1.8335t\\ &\longrightarrow 24-d=\sqrt[3]{13824-1.8335t}\\ &\longrightarrow d=24-\sqrt[3]{13824-1.8335t} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    A water tank in the shape of a horizontal cylinder with a radius of \(3\) m and a length of \(8\) m, as shown in Figure 49, is filling at a constant rate of \(1.1\text{m}^{3}/\text{min}\text{.}\) The goal of this problem is to approximate instantaneous rate of change numerically when the water is \(2\)m deep.
    🔗

    (a)

    Find the total volume of the tank in cubic meters.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} V &=\pi r^{2}h\\ &=\pi (3\text{m})^{2}(8\text{m})\\ &=72\pi\text{m}^{3} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the volume of water in the tank (in cubic meters) when the tank is filled to a depth of \(3\) m.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(V=\frac{1}{2}(72\pi\text{m}^{3})=36\pi\text{m}^{3}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    How long will it take for the tank to fill to a depth of \(3\) m if it starts empty?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\frac{36\pi\text{m}^{3}}{1.1\text{m}^{3}/\text{min}}\approx{}102.815\text{min} \)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    How long will it take for the tank to fill completely if it starts empty?
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\frac{72\pi\text{m}^{3}}{1.1\text{m}^{3}/\text{min}}\approx{}205.632\text{min} \)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (e)

    On the blank grid in Figure 50, sketch a graph of the depth of water as a function of time. Your sketch does not need to be perfect, but it should reflect how the rate of change of depth is changing as the tank fills.
    🔗
    You may assume the tank is empty to begin.
    🔗
    Solution.
    
    Depth as a Function of Time
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (f)

    The volume of water in the tank, in cubic meters, as a function of depth, \(h\text{,}\) in meters, is given by
    \begin{equation*} V(h)=8\left(h-3\right)\sqrt{9-\left(h-3\right)^2}+72\arcsin\left(\frac{h-3}{3}\right)+36\pi\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (i)

    Use the volume function, \(V(h)\text{,}\) to find the volume of water in the tank at a depth of \(1.9\) m and at a depth of \(2.1\) m.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} V(1.9) &=8\left(1.9-3\right)\sqrt{9-\left(1.9-3\right)^2}+72\arcsin\left(\frac{1.9-3}{3}\right)+36\pi\\ &=8\left(-1.1\right)\sqrt{9-\left(-1.1\right)^2}+72\arcsin\left(\frac{-1.1}{3}\right)+36\pi\\ &\approx{}61.5055\text{m}^{3} \end{align*}
    \begin{align*} V(2.1)&=8\left(2.1-3\right)\sqrt{9-\left(2.1-3\right)^2}+72\arcsin\left(\frac{2.1-3}{3}\right)+36\pi\\ &=8\left(-0.9\right)\sqrt{9-\left(-0.9\right)^2}+72\arcsin\left(\frac{-0.9}{3}\right)+36\pi\\ &\approx{}70.5544\text{m}^{3} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (ii)

    Use the fill rate to find how long it would take for the tank to fill from a depth of \(1.9\) m to a depth of \(2.1\) m.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The amount of water changed by \(70.5544-61.5055=+9.0489\text{m}^{3}\text{,}\) so it would take
    \begin{align*} \frac{9.0489\text{m}^{3}}{1.1\text{m}^{3}/\text{s}} &=8.2263\text{s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (iii)

    Calculate the average rate of change of depth as a function of time between the times when the depth is \(1.9\) m when the depth is \(2.1\) m.
    🔗
    Be careful! Your result should have units meters per minute!
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} \frac{\Delta{}h}{\Delta{}t} &=\frac{2.1\text{m}-1.9\text{m}}{8.2263\text{s}}\\ &\approx{}0.02431\text{m}/\text{s} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (g)

    Repeat the previous problem with depths of \(1.99\) and \(2.01\) meters.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} V(2.01)&\approx{}66.4545\text{m}^{3} & V(1.99)&\approx{}65.5494\text{m}^{3} \end{align*}
    so the amount of time it takes to fill from \(1.99\text{m}\) to \(2.01\text{m}\) is
    \begin{align*} \frac{(66.4545-65.5494)\text{m}^{3}}{1.1\text{m}^{3}/\text{s}} &=\frac{0.9051\text{m}^{3}}{1.1\text{m}^{3}/\text{s}}\\ &\approx{}0.8228\text{s} \end{align*}
    which means the rate at which the depth is rising is
    \begin{align*} \frac{(2.01-1.99)\text{m}}{0.8228\text{s}} &= \frac{0.02\text{m}}{0.8228\text{s}}\\ &=0.0243\text{m}/\text{s} \end{align*}
    🔗
