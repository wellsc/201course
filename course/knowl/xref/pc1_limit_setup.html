(ii)

Clearly explain why the limit you set up in Part a will find the derivative of \(s(t)\) at \(t=1\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
Using the two different ways we have defined the derivative, the limit to find \(s'(1)\text{,}\) the derivative of \(s\) at \(t=1\text{,}\) is
\begin{align*} s'(t) &= \lim_{t\rightarrow 1}\frac{s(t)-s(1)}{t-1}\\ \text{or}&\\ s'(t) &= \lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{s(1+h)-s(1)}{h}\\ &= \lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{s(1+h)-s(1)}{(1+h)-1}. \end{align*}
🔗
The expressions \(\frac{s(t)-s(1)}{t-1}\) for any value of \(t\) other than \(1\text{,}\) and \(\frac{s(1+h)-s(1)}{(1+h)-1}\text{,}\) for any non-zero value of \(h\text{,}\) are the average rate of change between the points \((1,s(1))\) and \((t,s(t))\text{,}\) or between \((1,s(1))\) and \((1+h,s(1+h))\text{,}\) respectively, so the closer \(t\) is to \(1\) or the closer \(h\) is to zero, the closer the average rate of change is to being an instantaneous rate of change.
