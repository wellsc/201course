Worksheet Exercise 4.

We will now express the volume as a function of depth.
🔗

(a)

Let \(d\) represent the depth of water as measured from the bottom of the tank. Use similar triangles to express the radius at a given depth in terms of \(d\text{.}\)
🔗
Answer.
\(r=\frac{1}{3}d\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
By similar triangles, \(\frac{r}{d}=\frac{2\text{m}}{6\text{m}}\text{,}\) so \(r=\frac{1}{3}d\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
🔗

(b)

Use the formula for the volume of a cone and your expression for the radius at a given depth to find the volume of (the cone of) water in the tank as a function of \(d\text{.}\)
🔗
Your expression should have the form \(V=G(d)\) where \(V\) is expressed in liters (convert it if your formula is for cubic meters!) \(d\) (in meters) is the only variable on the right hand side.
🔗
Answer.
Note that we are using a script V, \(\mathcal{V}(d)\) to differentiate it from \(V(t)\text{.}\)
🔗
\(\mathcal{V}(d)=\frac{1000\pi}{27}d^{3}\ell\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗
Solution.
\begin{align*} \mathcal{V}(d) \amp = \frac{1}{3}\pi{}r^{2} h\\ \amp = \frac{1}{3}\pi{}\left(\frac{d}{3}\right)^{2} d\text{m}^{3}\\ \amp = \frac{1}{3}\pi{}\frac{d^{2}}{9} d\text{m}^{3}\\ \amp = \frac{1}{27}\pi{}d^{3}\text{m}^{3}\\ \amp = \frac{1000}{27}\pi{}d^{3}\ell. \end{align*}
